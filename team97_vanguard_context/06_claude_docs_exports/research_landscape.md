# Financial Literacy Games — Landscape & Notes

Oct 10, 2026 · @Monishram Selvaraj

## TL;DR

Abroad, the best games make you live a month on a budget and feel the consequences. India mostly has comics, workbooks, a national test and classroom programs.

- **Global winners:** Spent, JA Finance Park, Banqer and the NGPF Arcade all run on choices → consequences → try again.
- **India:** lots of content (RBI comics, NCFE's national test, SEBI's app) and NGO classroom programs (AflaTeen, Svatah, Magic Bus). The only digital Indian sim we found, UBS + Pratham's Meri Dukaan, was a 2019 pilot.
- **Steal from four:** Spent (core loop), JA Finance Park (personas + volunteer-run sessions), Banqer / EVERFI (facilitator and sponsor dashboards), RBI's *Raju and the Forty Thieves* (40 real scam cases for the safety pillar).
- **Hidden rubric:** NCFE's own 2026 game challenge asked for offline, voice-over-text, decisions-not-quizzes designs. Vanguard's judges likely think the same way.
- **Local player:** Svatah, a Hyderabad NGO, already teaches money skills in government schools, but for Grades 6–9 on paper. Grades 11–12, digital, is open.

## Your notes, answered

Short answers to every question in your notes, in the order you wrote them.

### Is it just scaled with NGOs?

Yes. Vanguard pays, NGOs deliver. In India, companies spend CSR money (CSR = corporate social responsibility, the legally required social spending) through NGOs registered with the government on [Form CSR-1](https://cleartax.in/s/form-csr-1), required since April 2021. So scaling means **more NGOs × more centres × more batches**. What that means for the design:

- Onboarding a new NGO must be self-serve: create org → get join codes → run sessions.
- Adding a new language must be just adding text and audio files, not new code.
- It must be cheap to run, because CSR budgets get split across many programs.

### Needs vs wants: don't punish wants

If every "want" loses you points, students learn "never spend", which is wrong and nobody believes it. PAYBACK tracks **happiness and social life alongside money**. Do the same: a money meter and a happiness meter. The skill is balancing them, not saving everything.

### Financial goals + rewards

Yes. Small named goal → jar fills visibly → goal hit → the item unlocks in-game + a badge. This works because of labelling (next answer).

### How does a piggy bank actually work?

Three things, in order of strength:

1. **Labelling.** Money in a box marked "for X" feels off-limits. Economists call this *mental accounting*: we treat money differently depending on which mental "jar" we've put it in. In a [Kenya study](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/constraints-saving-health-expenditures-kenya), a simple locked box, with the key kept by the person, raised health savings by **66%**. A box with no key did no better, so labelling mattered more than being locked in.
2. **Friction.** Breaking or opening it takes effort, and the impulse to spend fades in that pause.
3. **Visibility.** You see and hear it fill up.

In our game: named jars ("Phone fund"), a small "sure?" pause before taking money out of a jar, and a jar that visibly fills.

### "Understand investing, age-appropriate": what does that mean?

Teach **why money grows and why risk exists**, using things they already know. No real products, no stock names, never "buy this". A 16-year-old can't really invest on their own anyway. The four ideas:

- **Risk:** the chance things turn out differently than you expected, including worse. A bigger possible reward usually means bigger risk.
- **Time horizon:** how long until you need the money. Money for next month and money for 10 years later should be treated differently.
- **Compounding:** growth on top of earlier growth (analogy below).
- **Diversification:** spreading money out so one failure doesn't sink you (analogy below).

### "Adapt to different levels of maturity": what does that mean?

Students have handled very different amounts of money. Roughly three levels:

| Level | Typical student | Game starts with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Only small pocket money, no UPI, no bank account | Small amounts, needs vs wants, saving |
| 2 | Uses family PhonePe/GPay, buys groceries for home, may get scholarship money | Monthly budget, digital safety, emergencies |
| 3 | Earns (shop, delivery, tuitions), adds to household income | Bigger amounts, family emergencies, investing chapter unlocked |

How: 3–4 picture-based questions at the start ("Do you get pocket money? Do you earn? Do you use UPI?") pick the persona and difficulty. The game then gets harder as they do well. Language and reading level adapt too.

### "NGO-integrated" (the wtf one)

The NGO is the delivery van. Vanguard won't hand an app to 1,500 kids directly; NGO staff and volunteers run sessions at their centres. So the NGO needs a **facilitator dashboard** that covers five jobs:

1. **Set up:** create batches, get a join code per batch (no student phone or email needed).
2. **Deliver:** unlock chapters for the class, a session guide any volunteer can follow, works on shared tablets.
3. **Track rollout:** how many students joined, are active, have finished, per centre.
4. **Support learning:** see where a batch is stuck ("60% failed the UPI scam level"), flag students who need help.
5. **Report:** export before/after scores for Vanguard.

### "Intuitive UI": what does it mean?

A student figures it out with nobody explaining. In practice:

- One main action per screen.
- Icons, pictures and audio alongside every bit of text.
- Big buttons that work on a cheap 5-inch Android.
- No sign-up form.
- Progress always visible.
- A guide character instead of tutorial screens.

The test: hand it to a 16-year-old, say nothing, and watch. Every question they ask is a UI bug.

### "Privacy-conscious": what does it mean?

The users are minors. India's DPDP Act (DPDP = Digital Personal Data Protection) treats under-18s as children. That means verifiable parental consent, and no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads aimed at them ([rules commentary](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule12.html)). In practice:

- Nickname + avatar only. No real name, phone, email or photo.
- Join through the NGO's code; the NGO collects consent.
- Never link a real bank account or UPI.
- No location tracking.
- Store progress, not personal life details.
- The NGO sees individual progress; Vanguard sees only totals.
- **The app must never ask for the things it teaches students to protect** (OTP, Aadhaar, PIN). Judges will notice if it does.

## Hyderabad analogies

Compounding = the Pillalamarri banyan. Diversification = a Charminar street vendor. Each one also gets a one-line version for teens.

### Compounding: the Pillalamarri banyan, not a snowball

A banyan's branches drop roots to the ground. Those roots thicken into new trunks, which grow new branches, which drop more roots. **Growth that makes more growth.** [Pillalamarri](https://mahabubnagar.nic.in/pillala-marri/) near Mahabubnagar is about 700 years old and spreads over roughly 3 acres from one tree. Its name means "children's banyan" in Telugu, which fits an app for students.

- **In the game:** each savings jar is a sapling. Every year in the simulation it drops a new root.
- **One-liner for teens:** a reel going viral. Each person who shares it brings in new viewers who share it again.

The maths, which the game shows as numbers, never as a promise:

```latex
A = P(1 + r)^t
```

Here A is what you end with, P is what you put in, r is the yearly growth rate, and t is the number of years. **Assuming** 8% a year (real returns vary and can be negative), ₹10,000 left alone for 30 years:

- If only the original ₹10,000 earns (no compounding): ₹34,000.
- With compounding: about ₹1,00,627.
- The ₹66,627 gap is growth earned by earlier growth.
- Leave it 40 years instead: about ₹2,17,245. Starting 10 years earlier roughly doubles the result.

### Diversification: the Charminar vendor, not the egg basket

Two vendors near Charminar. One sells only umbrellas: great in monsoon, nothing in April. The other sells umbrellas **and** caps and water bottles. When one item sells badly, the other sells well, so she earns in every season. Diversification means owning things that **don't fail at the same time**.

- **In the game:** a mini-game where you stock your stall before you know next month's weather.
- **One-liner for teens:** you don't pick 11 openers for SRH. A team of only big hitters collapses on a slow, turning pitch; batters + pacers + spinners means someone performs whatever the pitch.

## The gap: where we win

Nothing on this list does all six of these. Together, they're our pitch.

1. **Built for Intermediate students in Telangana.** Indian programs stop at Grade 9 (Svatah) or age 16 (AflaTeen).
2. **A digital simulation with consequences.** Indian options are comics, workbooks, tests and board games. The only digital sim, Meri Dukaan, was a 2019 pilot.
3. **Telugu, Urdu, Hindi and English, with voice.** The global sims are English-first.
4. **Indian scams as gameplay.** UPI PIN tricks, fake scholarship fees, task scams. Today those are pamphlets and ads.
5. **NGO dashboard + sponsor report built in.** Only EVERFI and Banqer do this, and neither is built for India.
6. **Zero real money needed.** Teen fintech apps run on a parent's money; ours can't.
